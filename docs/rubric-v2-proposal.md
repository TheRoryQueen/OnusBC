# Stricter grading: proposed rubric v2 (for Farnaz's approval)

Status, 3 October 2026: proposal only. Nothing has been graded, nothing has run, the live grades are
untouched. This is the first step of item 7 in docs/progress.md. No grading happens until Farnaz approves
this document, in writing, in the session.

## What changes from today's rubric (v1)

1. The same 17 criteria, the same 5 categories, the same ids and labels. The criteria table in the database
   keeps its rows; only the descriptions (the scoring guides) change.
2. The bar for full marks rises. v1 gave a 2 to any commitment that was "explicit and binding". v2 gives a 2
   only to a commitment that is explicit, specific and enforceable. A binding verb with no specifics a
   student could hold the school to is partial credit, not full credit.
3. Permissive or vague language caps a criterion at 1. The capped words: may, might, can (of the school),
   could, should, where possible, where practicable, as appropriate, endeavour, strive, seek to, aim to,
   generally, normally, as soon as possible, in a timely manner, case by case, and any wording that leaves
   the action to someone's discretion.
4. Missing is 0, as today. No credit by implication, no credit from outside knowledge.
5. Doubt resolves downward: if the evidence can carry two scores, the lower one wins.
6. The On paper grade moves from a 0 to 4 scale to a 0 to 100 score, with letters A at 80 and up, B 70 to 79,
   C 60 to 69, D 50 to 59, F under 50.
7. Nothing else about grading changes. Quotes are still checked word for word in code; a quote that is not
   found verbatim rejects and the criterion scores 0 as "Not found in policy". Where a school publishes
   separate procedures, policy and procedures grade together as one combined text. A login-only policy stays
   grey with Publicly posted and easy to find at 0. Grades stay precomputed and stored, never live.

## The scoring rules the auditor follows

For every criterion, one score:

- **2, full credit.** The commitment passes all three tests. Explicit: stated directly in the text, not
  inferred. Specific: concrete enough that a student could hold the school to it (a named office or role, a
  defined step, a number, a deadline). Enforceable: binding on the institution, in "will", "must", "shall" or
  a present-tense obligation, not an aspiration or a discretion.
- **1, partial credit.** The criterion is addressed but the commitment is vague, permissive, discretionary,
  or binding without specifics. This is where every "may", "where possible", "endeavour" and "as
  appropriate" lands, at most.
- **0.** Not addressed, or addressed only by wording that works against the criterion.

## The maths

- Each criterion still scores 0, 1 or 2. A school's maximum is 34 points across the 17.
- Category score = points earned in the category divided by points possible in the category, times 100.
- The On paper score is the mean of the five category scores, rounded to a whole number; a half rounds down,
  the stricter way.
- Letters: A 80 and up, B 70 to 79, C 60 to 69, D 50 to 59, F under 50.
- Worked example. A school earns 5 of 6 in Accessible (83), 6 of 10 in Survivor rights (60), 4 of 6 in
  Process (67), 5 of 8 in Accountability (63), 2 of 4 in Training (50). Mean of the five: 64.6, rounds to 65,
  letter C.

## The In practice question (one decision needed)

The PRD puts both grades on one scale, and the gap is the plain distance between them. Moving On paper to
0 to 100 forces a choice:

- **Option A, recommended.** In practice also moves to 0 to 100: every answer's 0 to 4 value becomes 0 to 100
  by times 25, same weights, same blocks. One shared scale survives, the gap stays a plain difference, and
  one set of letter bands serves both grades. Side effect to accept: an In practice letter gets easier to
  reach (an A at 80 instead of today's 3.5 out of 4, which is 87.5).
- **Option B.** On paper alone moves to 0 to 100; In practice stays 0 to 4 and every place the two meet
  divides On paper by 25 first. The site then carries two scales with two letter rules, which is exactly
  what the PRD's shared scale was built to avoid.

The gap labels rescale with whichever option wins: aligned within 12.5 points, some gap up to 37.5, big gap
above that, better in practice mirrored (these are today's 0.5 and 1.5 on the 0 to 4 scale, times 25).

## Expected effect (an estimate, not a run)

Today's 25 graded schools sit at B (6 schools) and C (19) on the 0 to 4 scale. Read through the new bands
alone, with no re-grading, those same scores would land B (3), C (4), D (11), F (7). The v2 re-grade will sit
below that line, because permissive quotes lose full credit: of today's 143 verified score-2 lines, 20 quote
wording that v2 caps at 1, and the specificity test will catch more that the word scan cannot see. Expect
most of the province in D and F and only the strongest policies near B. That is what a stricter auditor
means; flagging it now so the map's colour spread and the Devpost story are not a surprise.
